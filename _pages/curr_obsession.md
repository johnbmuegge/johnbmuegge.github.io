Spoken word recognition (and to some extent, speech perception) are largely characterized as processes which closely follow the temporal sequence of the input. For instance, it is well accepted that people begin to use speech information to make inferences about the identity of a spoken word *immediately* upon perception, and that these inferences are updated *incrementally* as soon as new information arrives. This proposes that the system of spoken word recognition is strongly tied to the timing of when speech input arrives--an assumption that is embedded in many computational models. 

However, over the past ~15 years research has found that the system does not always work this way.
