At the broadest level I am interested in understanding the mechanisms which support language comprehension, how individuals differ in these systems, and what aspects of their ontological histories lead to this variance. This includes research in how listeners leverage acoustic cues to make judegments about speech identity, how spoken words are recognized (in and out of noisy listening contexts), how learning a second language affects these processes, how listeners with hearing loss (particularly those treated with a cochlear implant) adapt to these challenges, and how children develop adult-like language skills. 

I believe that understanding the richness found across listeners in how language comprehension is achieved requires a multi-dimensional approach. To this end I use tasks ranging from simple rating scales to eye-tracking, and complement this with neuroimaging techniques and computational modeling. 

I am currently a Postdoctoral Fellow at the University of Connecticut, working with Drs. [Jim Magnuson](https://psychology.uconn.edu/person/james-magnuson/) and [Emily Myers](https://myerslab.uconn.edu/). You can reach me at [john.muegge@uconn.edu](mailto:john.muegge@uconn.edu).
